For Patients

Before and After Your Surgery

This page covers what my patients usually ask before their first visit and around knee arthroscopy. It is a general guide: you will get written instructions for your own case, and those are the ones to follow.

For your first visit

  • Your ID or passport.
  • X-rays, MRI or CT scans with their reports. If you have them on a CD or a link, bring those too.
  • A list of the medicines you take, with doses, and any drug allergies.
  • Past surgeries and medical conditions.
  • Comfortable clothes: shorts if you are coming for your knee, or a sleeveless top if you are coming for your shoulder.

It helps to write down when and how the problem started, what makes it worse or better, and the questions you want to ask.

Before surgery

We will order blood tests and, depending on your age and health, an EKG and an anesthesia evaluation. The anesthesia team will tell you how long to fast.

Tell us if you take blood thinners, aspirin, clopidogrel, anti-inflammatory medicines, diabetes medicines or supplements. I will tell you which ones to stop and when. Do not stop any of them on your own.

If you have a cut, scrape or skin infection on the knee, or a fever in the days before, let me know: the date may need to change.

Get your home ready: crutches if they were prescribed, cold packs or ice, loose clothing, and someone to go with you. You cannot drive after anesthesia.

On the day of surgery

Arrive at the scheduled time, without jewelry, makeup or nail polish. Bring your imaging, your crutches if prescribed and your medicine list. Most patients go home the same day or the next.

The first days at home

  • Keep the leg raised when sitting or lying down.
  • Apply cold for 15 to 20 minutes several times a day, with a cloth between the ice and your skin.
  • Take your pain medicines as prescribed. Take anti-inflammatory medicines with food.
  • Keep the dressing clean and dry. Cover it to shower until you are told the wound can get wet.
  • Put weight on the leg and use crutches as instructed: it is not the same after a meniscus trimming as after a meniscus repair or a ligament reconstruction.
  • Move your ankle and tighten your thigh muscle several times a day. These exercises help prevent blood clots and rebuild strength.

Some swelling and bruising of the knee and leg is normal in the first days.

Follow-up visits and physical therapy

The first follow-up and stitch removal usually happen between 10 and 14 days. Physical therapy starts according to the procedure, sometimes in the first week, and moves in stages. Returning to work, driving and sports depends on what was done and is cleared at your follow-up visits.

Warning signs

Message me on WhatsApp right away if you have a fever of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or higher, spreading redness, drainage from the wound, pain that does not ease with your medicine, swelling that keeps increasing, calf pain or swelling, or a cold, pale or tingling foot. If you have shortness of breath or chest pain, go to the emergency room.

Frequently asked questions

Before your appointment

When can I shower?

You can shower with the dressing well covered. Let the wound get wet only when you are told, and do not soak it in a bathtub, pool or the ocean until it has healed.

Is it normal for the knee to swell after arthroscopy?

Yes, some swelling and bruising is normal in the first days. Let us know if the swelling increases a lot, if you have a fever or if your calf hurts.

When can I drive?

When you can brake hard without pain and are no longer taking pain medicines that make you drowsy. It depends on which leg was operated on and the procedure, and is cleared at your follow-up.

When can I go back to work?

Office work usually within a few days to a few weeks. Physical work takes longer. We set it based on the procedure and your recovery.
