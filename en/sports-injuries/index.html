Sports Injuries

Request an appointment on WhatsApp

Sports injuries include sprains, tendinitis, muscle tears, overuse injuries, knee injuries and, sometimes, a fracture. The first step is a full assessment: how the injury happened, what it keeps the patient from doing, and what tests they already have.

Dr. Alberto Sadun Rodríguez is Head of the Department of Orthopedics at UEESCLINIC Teaching Hospital in Samborondón. He trained at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem and is a medical graduate of Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil. His practice focuses on the knee: the anterior cruciate ligament, the meniscus and cartilage, using arthroscopy when it is indicated.

Symptoms

Sudden pain when pivoting, jumping, stopping or taking a blow, sometimes with a pop; swelling; difficulty putting weight on the foot; a feeling that the knee or ankle gives way; or locking of the knee. Pain that appears with a repeated sporting movement and eases with rest is more typical of overuse or tendinitis. Deformity, inability to move a limb or a wound require immediate care.

When to seek care

It is a good idea to be seen if pain, swelling or instability keeps you from training or walking normally, or if symptoms are not improving after a few days of care. Seek care immediately if you felt a pop with significant swelling, you cannot put weight on the leg, the joint locks, there is a deformity or a wound, or the limb becomes cold, pale or numb.

Diagnosis

The appointment includes the mechanism of injury, the sport you play and a focused exam. X-rays help rule out a fracture. Magnetic resonance imaging is requested when a ligament, meniscus or other soft-tissue injury is suspected and would change the plan. Not every injury needs it from the first visit.

Treatment options

Many injuries improve with initial protection, progressive loading and movement, physical therapy and a temporary change in activity. Complete, prolonged rest is not the rule for every soft-tissue injury. In the knee, arthroscopy may be considered for an anterior cruciate ligament injury with instability that limits sports or daily life, or for a meniscus tear with true locking, a repairable tear, or pain and giving way that did not improve with non-surgical treatment. If there is a fracture, the plan may be immobilization or internal fixation. Not every sports injury needs surgery.

What recovery is like, in general terms

A sprain is not handled the same way as tendinitis, and a knee arthroscopy is not the same as a fracture. Clearance to return to sport comes from your doctor, not from the disappearance of pain alone. There is no single timeline that fits everyone. Report fever, spreading redness, pain out of proportion, swelling that gets worse, or calf pain and swelling.

Before your appointment

Does every sports injury need surgery?

No. Many improve with physical therapy and a change in activity. Surgery is recommended when the assessment shows it can help, or when non-surgical treatment has not been enough.

Can I go back to training once the pain is gone?

Not always. The disappearance of pain is not, by itself, clearance to return to sport. Return is authorized when motion, strength and stability are adequate for that sport.

Does Dr. Sadun treat sports injuries of the knee?

Yes. His practice focuses on knee surgery and arthroscopy, especially the anterior cruciate ligament, the meniscus and cartilage. He also assesses sprains, tendinitis, muscle tears, overuse injuries and sports-related fractures.
