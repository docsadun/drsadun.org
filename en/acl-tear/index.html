Knee Arthroscopy

ACL Tear

The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) sits in the center of the knee and keeps the shinbone from sliding forward. It gives the knee stability when you pivot, stop or change direction. An ACL tear is one of the most common sports injuries and one of the ones I see most often.

Dr. Alberto Sadun Rodríguez is an orthopedic surgeon with a special focus on knee arthroscopy and Head of the Orthopedic Surgery Department at UEESCLINIC Teaching Hospital in Samborondón. He trained at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem and keeps up his knee arthroscopy training through Smith+Nephew labs in Andover, Massachusetts.

How it happens and what it feels like

It usually tears when you twist with the foot planted, land from a jump or stop suddenly, often without contact with another player. It is common in soccer, basketball, padel and skiing. Many people feel or hear a pop, have to stop playing, and the knee swells over the next few hours.

Once the swelling goes down, the knee may feel almost normal walking in a straight line but gives way when you turn, go down stairs or change direction. It often comes with injuries to the meniscus, the cartilage or the medial collateral ligament.

When to seek care

See a doctor soon if you felt a pop followed by quick swelling or if the knee gives way. Get care right away if you cannot put weight on the leg, the knee looks deformed or is locked and will not straighten, or if you develop calf pain and swelling or shortness of breath.

Diagnosis

The physical exam, with tests that measure how much the shinbone moves forward, tells a lot. X-rays rule out fractures, and an MRI confirms the tear and shows any meniscus or cartilage injury, which changes the treatment plan.

Treatment without surgery

Not every ACL tear needs surgery. For people who do not play pivoting sports and do not have instability, a well-guided physical therapy program with strengthening and balance work can give a stable knee for daily life. If instability shows up later, the reconstruction can still be done.

Arthroscopic reconstruction

Reconstruction is recommended when there is instability, when you want to return to pivoting sports, or when there are associated injuries worth repairing. Through small incisions, the torn ligament is replaced with a graft, usually one of your own tendons (hamstrings, patellar tendon or quadriceps tendon). We discuss which graft fits your case at the visit.

Before surgery, the knee should have its motion back and the swelling should be down. Going into surgery with good motion and strength makes recovery easier.

What recovery is like, in general terms

Most patients go home the same day or the next. You use crutches at first and start physical therapy within the first few days. Recovery moves in stages: motion, strength, jogging, agility drills and, finally, sport.

Returning to pivoting sports usually takes at least nine months and is cleared only after strength and function testing. Rushing back raises the risk of tearing it again. Let us know if you have fever, spreading redness, pain out of proportion, calf pain or swelling, or shortness of breath.

Frequently asked questions

Before your appointment

Does every ACL tear need surgery?

No. Some people get a stable knee back with physical therapy and a change in activity. Reconstruction is considered when there is instability, when you want to return to pivoting sports, or when there are associated injuries.

What happens if I skip surgery and keep playing?

If the knee keeps giving way, each episode can damage the meniscus and the cartilage. So if there is instability, it is not a good idea to go back to pivoting sports without treating it.

What graft is used?

Almost always one of your own tendons: hamstrings, patellar tendon or quadriceps tendon. The choice depends on your age, your sport and your knee, and we go over it at the visit.

How long until I can play sports again?

In general, at least nine months for pivoting sports, and only after strength and function testing. Walking and daily activities come back much sooner.
