Osteoporosis

Request an appointment on WhatsApp

Osteoporosis is the loss of bone mass and strength. It does not hurt on its own, which is why it is called silent: it is often found only after a fracture from a minor fall or bump, especially of the wrist, hip or spine.

Dr. Alberto Sadun Rodríguez is an orthopedic surgeon and Head of the Department of Orthopedics at UEESCLINIC Teaching Hospital in Samborondón. He trained at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem and is a medical graduate of Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil.

Who is at higher risk

It is more common in women after menopause and increases with age. A family history of hip fracture, long-term steroid use, smoking, alcohol, low body weight, inactivity and some conditions, such as rheumatoid arthritis or thyroid problems, also play a role.

Diagnosis

It is diagnosed with a bone density scan (DEXA), a quick, painless test that measures bone density at the hip and spine. It is recommended for women from age 65, men from age 70, and earlier if there are risk factors or a fracture from a minor fall. Blood tests, such as vitamin D, are ordered as needed.

Treatment and prevention

Treatment combines weight-bearing and strengthening exercise, calcium and vitamin D as prescribed and, when appropriate, medicines that strengthen the bone. Preventing falls matters just as much: good lighting, removing loose rugs, grab bars in the bathroom, sturdy shoes and reviewing medicines that cause dizziness.

After a fracture

Any fracture from a minor fall is a warning. Besides treating the fracture, osteoporosis needs to be tested for and treated, because the risk of a second fracture in the following years is high.

When to seek care

Book a visit if you have risk factors, if you have had a fracture from a minor fall, or if you have lost height or notice your back curving more. Go to the emergency room if after a fall you cannot put weight on your leg or have severe hip pain.

Before your appointment

Does osteoporosis hurt?

No. What hurts is the fracture it can cause, so it is worth finding early with a bone density scan.

When should I get a bone density scan?

Women from age 65 and men from age 70, and earlier with risk factors, long-term steroid use or a fracture from a minor fall.

What fractures does osteoporosis cause?

The most common are wrist, hip and spine fractures. Spine fractures can go unnoticed or be mistaken for ordinary back pain.

Can it be prevented?

Yes. Weight-bearing exercise, enough calcium in your diet, vitamin D as prescribed, not smoking, limiting alcohol and preventing falls all lower the risk of fracture.
