What knee arthroscopy looks like

I enter the knee with a camera through two or three small incisions. Watching the screen, I repair the meniscus and ligaments without opening the joint.

You usually go home the same day or the next. Recovery time depends on the injury and the procedure.

Dr. Alberto Sadun

Orthopedic Surgeon

I trained in orthopedic surgery at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem, Israel. I earned my medical degree at Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil.

Before my specialty training, I completed my internship at Jackson Memorial Hospital through the William J. Harrington Program for Latin America. I then served as research director in Dr. Carlos Lavernia's Division of Arthritis Surgery at the University of Miami, preparing articles and papers for medical journals and meetings.

I have operated in Guayaquil and Samborondón since 2006, and since August 2021 I have been Head of the Department of Orthopedics at UEESCLINIC. My practice focuses on arthroscopic knee surgery: the anterior cruciate ligament, meniscus and cartilage. I also treat shoulder fractures, dislocations and injuries, other fractures and sports injuries.

I am Adjunct Faculty of the William J. Harrington Training Program.

Before your appointment

When should I see an orthopedic surgeon?
  • If you suffer an injury, a fall or an accident, seek care immediately.
  • If pain in a joint, muscle or bone starts suddenly with no obvious injury and lasts more than 3 days.
  • If there is swelling, if you cannot put weight on your foot or move your arm, or if you felt a pop when you got hurt.

An early diagnosis keeps the injury from getting worse.

What should I bring to my appointment?

Your ID or passport, any tests you have (X-rays, MRIs, ultrasounds and their reports) and a list of the medicines you take. If you have insurance, bring your policy details: we fill out the forms for your reimbursement.

What is arthroscopy?

It is surgery done through two or three small incisions, using a camera to see inside the joint and repair the meniscus, ligaments or cartilage. Patients usually go home the same day or the next.
