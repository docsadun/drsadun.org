Osteoarthritis

Osteoarthritis is the gradual wear of the cartilage that covers the ends of the bones inside a joint. It is the most common cause of joint pain from middle age on and mostly affects the knee, the hip, the hands and the spine.

Dr. Alberto Sadun Rodríguez is an orthopedic surgeon and Head of the Department of Orthopedics at UEESCLINIC Teaching Hospital in Samborondón. He trained at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem and is a medical graduate of Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil.

Why it happens

Age, excess weight, genetics, high-load work or sports, and past joint injuries, such as a meniscus or ligament tear or a fracture that involved the cartilage, all play a role.

Symptoms

Pain that gets worse with activity and better with rest, stiffness on getting up or after sitting that lasts a few minutes, creaking and, over time, swelling, loss of motion or deformity. With the knee, stairs become hard; with the hip, putting on socks or shoes is difficult and the pain is often felt in the groin.

Diagnosis

It is based on the history, an exam of the joint and an X-ray, which for the knee is taken standing to see how much space is left between the bones. An MRI is usually not needed to diagnose osteoarthritis.

Treatment without surgery

This is the foundation and most people improve with it: exercise and muscle strengthening, weight control, physical therapy, pain relievers or anti-inflammatory medicines when appropriate (always with food) and aids such as a cane. Injections are used in some cases. Arthroscopy is not recommended to treat osteoarthritis itself.

When joint replacement is recommended

When pain and limitation can no longer be controlled with well-done treatment and affect sleep, walking or daily activities, a knee or hip replacement can restore quality of life. The decision is made together with you, based on symptoms, X-rays, age and overall health.

When to seek care

Book a visit if joint pain limits your activities, if stiffness or swelling does not settle, or if the joint is becoming deformed. Get care right away if the joint becomes hot, red and very swollen with a fever.

Frequently asked questions

Before your appointment

Can osteoarthritis be cured?

Worn cartilage does not grow back, but symptoms can be controlled very well with exercise, weight control, physical therapy and medicines. When that is not enough, joint replacement is an effective option.

Does exercise make osteoarthritis worse?

No. The right exercise and muscle strengthening reduce pain and protect the joint. The key is choosing the right type of exercise, and physical therapy helps with that.

Does arthroscopy help osteoarthritis?

Not as a treatment for osteoarthritis itself. It may be considered only in selected cases, for example when there is also a meniscus tear locking the knee.

When is a joint replacement needed?

When pain and limitation persist despite nonsurgical treatment and affect daily life. It is decided together, case by case.
