Hip Pain

Hip pain may come from osteoarthritis, from tendon or bursa irritation, from pain that starts in the back, or from a fracture, especially after a fall. The first step is to identify the cause.

Symptoms

Pain in the groin, the side of the hip or the buttock, which may travel down the thigh; stiffness when starting to walk; difficulty walking, climbing stairs or putting on shoes. After a fall, severe pain, inability to put weight on the leg or the feeling that one leg is shorter require immediate care.

When to seek care

It is a good idea to be seen if the pain limits walking, work or rest. Seek care immediately after a fall if you cannot put weight on the leg, the hip looks deformed, there is a wound, there is fever with a hot and very painful joint, or the foot becomes pale, cold or numb.

Diagnosis

The assessment includes how the pain started and an exam of the hip and, when needed, of the back and the knee. The first study is usually an X-ray. Magnetic resonance imaging or other tests are requested if a fracture that does not show on the X-ray is suspected, or if another cause needs more detail.

Treatment options

For osteoarthritis, the first plan is usually physical therapy, pain relief as your doctor directs, activity changes and, when appropriate, walking support. Total hip replacement may be considered when pain and limitation persist despite that treatment and imaging supports the decision. The choice is made with you.

A hip fracture in an adult is, in most cases, treated with surgery once the assessment is complete. The type of procedure depends on where the fracture is and on the patient's overall health. Do not delay seeking care if a fracture is suspected.

What recovery is like, in general terms

It is not the same with osteoarthritis managed without surgery, with a total hip replacement or with a fracture. It includes pain control, a walking plan and physical therapy. There is no single timeline that fits everyone. Report fever, spreading redness, pain out of proportion, swelling that gets worse, calf pain or swelling, or shortness of breath.

Frequently asked questions

Before your appointment

Does every case of hip osteoarthritis end in a replacement?

No. For many patients the first plan is conservative. Replacement is considered when pain and limitation remain significant despite that treatment.

What is a total hip replacement?

It is surgery in which the damaged surfaces of the joint are replaced with an implant, after a full assessment, not on the X-ray alone.

Does a hip fracture always need surgery?

In most adults, yes, once the assessment is complete. If you fell and cannot put weight on the leg, seek care immediately.
