Back and Neck Pain

Back pain is one of the most common reasons people see a doctor. Most of the time it comes from a mechanical problem in the muscles, discs or joints of the spine and improves within a few weeks. At the visit I assess neck, mid-back and lower back pain looking for the cause, so we treat more than the symptom.

Dr. Alberto Sadun Rodríguez is an orthopedic surgeon and Head of the Orthopedic Surgery Department at UEESCLINIC Teaching Hospital in Samborondón. He trained at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem and is a medical graduate of Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil.

Common causes

Muscle strains from effort, poor posture or inactivity; disc wear (degenerative disc disease) and herniated discs; arthritis of the spinal joints; narrowing of the canal the nerves run through (stenosis); scoliosis; and vertebral fractures from osteoporosis, which can happen with very little effort.

Symptoms

Pain in the neck, mid-back or lower back, stiffness and trouble moving. When a nerve is pinched, the pain travels down the leg (sciatica) or the arm and may come with tingling, numbness or weakness.

When to seek care

Go to the emergency room right away if your legs are getting weaker, the area between your legs feels numb, or you lose control of your bladder or bowels. Also get urgent care if the pain comes with fever, after a significant fall, if you have osteoporosis or a history of cancer, or if you are losing weight without explanation.

Book a visit if the pain lasts more than four to six weeks, travels down your leg or arm, wakes you up at night or keeps coming back.

Diagnosis

The history and physical exam, including strength, reflexes and sensation, point to the cause in most cases. Imaging is not always needed at first. X-rays and MRI are ordered when there are warning signs, when the pain does not improve with treatment, or when a nerve is compressed.

Treatment

Staying active helps more than bed rest. Treatment usually combines pain relievers or anti-inflammatory medicines when appropriate (always with food), heat, physical therapy and an exercise program to strengthen the core and back and improve posture. Injections are used in selected cases.

If the cause needs spine surgery, for example a herniated disc with weakness, we explain the indication and arrange the next step.

Frequently asked questions

Before your appointment

Do I need an MRI for back pain?

Not always. Most back pain gets better without imaging. An MRI is ordered when there are warning signs, pain down the leg or arm with weakness, or pain that does not improve with treatment.

Is bed rest better?

No. A few days of reduced activity can help, but keeping moving and getting back to your usual activities soon speeds recovery.

What is sciatica?

It is pain that runs down the back of the leg when the sciatic nerve is irritated, almost always by a herniated disc in the lower spine. It often improves without surgery.

When is back pain an emergency?

When your legs are getting weaker, the area between your legs is numb, or you lose bladder or bowel control. Also if it comes with fever or starts after a significant fall.
