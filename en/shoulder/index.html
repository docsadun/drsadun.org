Shoulder fractures

For the shoulder, I treat fractures only. I do not perform shoulder arthroscopy or shoulder replacement.

Shoulder fractures

They are almost always caused by a fall or a blow. The upper end of the arm bone (proximal humerus), the clavicle (collarbone) or, less often, the shoulder blade can break. They cause severe pain, swelling, bruising and trouble lifting the arm; with a clavicle fracture you may feel a bump under the skin.

Many are treated with a sling, pain control and gradual movement. Internal fixation (surgery to hold the bone with plates, screws or nails) is considered when the fracture is displaced or unstable, there is an open wound, or an injury to the blood vessels or nerves of the arm, or nonsurgical care cannot keep acceptable alignment. Not every shoulder fracture needs surgery.

When to seek care

Get care right away if after a fall the shoulder looks deformed or you cannot move the arm, if there is an open wound, or if the hand turns cold, pale or numb. Left shoulder pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating may come from the heart: go to the emergency room.

Book an appointment if, after a fall or a blow, pain, swelling or trouble moving the arm suggest a fracture of the shoulder, collarbone or shoulder blade.

Diagnosis

The visit covers how the problem started, an exam of the shoulder, and a check of strength, circulation and sensation in the arm. The first study is usually an X-ray, and a CT scan is used for some fractures.

What recovery is like, in general terms

It depends on the injury and the treatment, and includes physical therapy once your doctor clears it. There is no single timeline that fits everyone. Let us know if you have fever, spreading redness, pain out of proportion, swelling that gets worse, tingling or a pale hand, or trouble moving your fingers.

Frequently asked questions

Before your appointment

Does every clavicle fracture need surgery?

No. Many are treated with immobilization and follow-up. Surgery is considered when there is significant displacement or other indications explained at the appointment.

Does every shoulder fracture need surgery?

No. Many are treated with a sling, pain control and gradual movement. Internal fixation (surgery to hold the bone with plates, screws or nails) is considered when the fracture is displaced or unstable, there is an open wound, or an injury to the blood vessels or nerves of the arm, or nonsurgical care cannot keep acceptable alignment.
