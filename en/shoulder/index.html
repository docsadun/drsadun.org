Shoulder: fractures, dislocations and injuries

Request an appointment on WhatsApp

The shoulder is the most mobile joint in the body, which is also why it is one of the most often injured. In the office I treat fractures, dislocations and muscle and ligament injuries of the shoulder.

Dr. Alberto Sadun Rodríguez is Head of the Department of Orthopedics at UEESCLINIC Teaching Hospital in Samborondón. He trained at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem and is a medical graduate of Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil.

Shoulder fractures

They are almost always caused by a fall or a blow. The upper end of the arm bone (proximal humerus), the clavicle (collarbone) or, less often, the shoulder blade can break. They cause severe pain, swelling, bruising and trouble lifting the arm; with a clavicle fracture you may feel a bump under the skin.

Many are treated with a sling, pain control and gradual movement. Internal fixation (surgery to hold the bone with plates, screws or nails) is considered when the fracture is displaced or unstable, there is an open wound, or an injury to the blood vessels or nerves of the arm, or nonsurgical care cannot keep acceptable alignment. Not every shoulder fracture needs surgery.

Shoulder dislocation

What it is

A dislocation happens when the ball of the arm bone slips out of the socket, almost always toward the front, after a fall on an outstretched arm or a hit during sports. It is very painful, the shoulder looks deformed and moving the arm is very difficult.

Urgency and reduction

Do not try to put it back. Hold the arm against your body in the least painful position and go to the UEESCLINIC emergency room. Let us know on WhatsApp that you are on your way. The reduction is done after an X-ray.

Immobilization

After the reduction, the arm is kept in a sling from a few days to a few weeks, depending on age and the injury, to protect the joint while the swelling settles.

Rehabilitation

Physical therapy then begins, with a plan for movement and strength that is adjusted for each person. In young people and in athletes, the risk of the shoulder coming out again is high. If it stays unstable or dislocates more than once, surgical treatment is considered when appropriate.

Muscle and ligament injuries of the shoulder

A blow, a fall or an effort can injure the muscles or ligaments of the shoulder without breaking the bone. In the office I see sprains, strains, acromioclavicular joint injuries and contusions.

A sprain is a stretch or a tear of a ligament. A strain is an injury to a muscle or its tendon from a stretch or an effort. At the acromioclavicular joint, where the collarbone meets the acromion, the upper part of the shoulder blade, a direct blow on the shoulder can separate those surfaces: it hurts, it swells and sometimes a bump can be felt. A contusion is the damage from a direct blow, with pain and bruising, without the bone coming out of place or breaking.

The diagnosis starts from how the injury happened and from the exam. An X-ray helps rule out a fracture or a dislocation. Ultrasound or magnetic resonance imaging is used to see muscles and ligaments when the exam calls for it.

Most are treated without surgery: relative rest, ice in the first few days, pain medicine when it is indicated, a short period of immobilization if needed, and rehabilitation. Surgical treatment is considered when appropriate.

When to seek care

Get care right away if after a fall the shoulder looks deformed or you cannot move the arm, if there is an open wound, or if the hand turns cold, pale or numb. Left shoulder pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating may come from the heart: go to the emergency room.

Book an appointment if, after a fall or a blow, pain, swelling or trouble moving the arm suggest a fracture of the shoulder, collarbone or shoulder blade. Also book if the shoulder came out, if it feels loose, or if a sprain, a strain, a contusion or an acromioclavicular joint injury is not getting better.

Diagnosis

The visit covers how the problem started, an exam of the shoulder, and a check of strength, circulation and sensation in the arm. The first study is usually an X-ray, and a computed tomography scan is used for some fractures. Ultrasound or magnetic resonance imaging helps assess muscles and ligaments.

What recovery is like, in general terms

It depends on the injury and the treatment, and includes physical therapy once your doctor clears it. There is no single timeline that fits everyone. Let us know if you have fever, spreading redness, pain out of proportion, swelling that gets worse, tingling or a pale hand, or trouble moving your fingers.

Before your appointment

Does every clavicle fracture need surgery?

No. Many are treated with immobilization and follow-up. Surgery is considered when there is significant displacement or other indications explained at the appointment.

Does every shoulder fracture need surgery?

No. Many are treated with a sling, pain control and gradual movement. Internal fixation (surgery to hold the bone with plates, screws or nails) is considered when the fracture is displaced or unstable, there is an open wound, or an injury to the blood vessels or nerves of the arm, or nonsurgical care cannot keep acceptable alignment.

What should I do if my shoulder comes out?

Do not try to put it back. Hold the arm against your body in the least painful position and go to the UEESCLINIC emergency room. Let us know on WhatsApp that you are on your way. The reduction is done after an X-ray.

Does every muscle or ligament injury of the shoulder need surgery?

No. Sprains, strains, contusions and many acromioclavicular joint injuries are treated with relative rest, pain control and rehabilitation.
