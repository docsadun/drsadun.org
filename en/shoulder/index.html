Shoulder Fractures

A shoulder fracture is, usually after a fall or a blow, a break of the upper end of the arm bone (proximal humerus), the clavicle (collarbone) or, less often, the shoulder blade.

Dr. Alberto Sadun Rodríguez is Head of the Department of Orthopedics and Traumatology at UEESCLINIC Teaching Hospital in Samborondón. He trained at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem and is a medical graduate of Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil.

Symptoms

Severe pain, swelling, bruising or deformity in the shoulder, collarbone or upper arm; difficulty lifting the arm; with a clavicle fracture, sometimes a bump under the skin. Tingling, paleness or loss of strength in the hand should be assessed immediately.

When to seek care

Any suspected shoulder fracture should be assessed promptly. Seek care immediately if there is a deformity, an open wound, the arm or hand becomes cold, pale or numb, you cannot move your fingers, or the pain is out of proportion and the arm feels very tight.

Diagnosis

The appointment includes how the injury happened, an exam of the shoulder and the arm, and a check of circulation and sensation in the hand. The first study is usually an X-ray; in some cases a computed tomography scan is requested.

Treatment options

Many clavicle and proximal humerus fractures are treated with a sling, pain control and gradual mobilization. Internal fixation (surgery to hold the bone with plates, screws or nails) may be considered when the fracture is displaced, unstable, there is an open wound, or non-surgical treatment does not allow acceptable alignment. The decision is made with you, according to the bone, the fracture pattern, your age and your activity. The goal is to treat the fracture and help recover movement, with a plan explained without rushing. Not every shoulder fracture needs surgery.

What recovery is like, in general terms

It depends on the bone, the type of fracture and the treatment, and includes physical therapy once your doctor clears you. There is no single timeline that fits everyone. Report fever, spreading redness, pain out of proportion, swelling that gets worse, tingling or paleness of the hand, or trouble moving your fingers.

Frequently asked questions

Before your appointment

Does every clavicle fracture need surgery?

No. Many are treated with immobilization and follow-up. Surgery is considered when there is significant displacement or other indications explained at the appointment.

What is a proximal humerus fracture?

It is a break in the upper end of the arm bone, at the shoulder. Treatment may be non-surgical or with internal fixation, depending on the assessment.

Is recovery the same for everyone?

No. The timeline depends on the type of fracture, your age, your health and rehabilitation.
