Fractures

Book on WhatsApp

A fracture is a break in a bone, from a fall, an accident, a blow or, in weakened bone, a more minor injury. Some are managed with a cast or a splint; others need internal fixation to align and stabilize the bone.

Dr. Alberto Sadun Rodríguez is Head of the Department of Orthopedics at UEESCLINIC Teaching Hospital in Samborondón. He trained at Hadassah Hebrew University Hospital in Jerusalem and is a medical graduate of Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil.

Symptoms

Severe pain at the site of the blow or fall, swelling, bruising or deformity, and inability or great difficulty using the arm or putting weight on the foot. Tingling, paleness, coldness, loss of movement beyond the injury or a wound near the bone require immediate care.

When to seek care

If you suspect a fracture, seek care immediately. Get urgent care if there is a deformity, an open wound, you cannot put weight on the limb or move it, the pain is out of proportion and the area feels very tight, or the fingers or toes become cold, pale, numb or will not move. There are specific pages on this site for the shoulder and the hip.

Diagnosis

The appointment includes how the injury happened, an exam of the limb, and a check of circulation, sensation and movement beyond the fracture. The first study is usually an X-ray; in some cases a computed tomography scan is requested. If the X-ray is normal but the clinical picture is convincing, immobilization and a follow-up or a further study may be indicated.

Treatment options

Many fractures are treated with a cast, a splint or other immobilization, and a plan for weight bearing or movement according to the bone. Internal fixation may be considered when the fracture is displaced, unstable, involves a joint surface, there is an open wound, or non-surgical treatment does not allow acceptable alignment. The decision is made with you. Not every fracture needs surgery.

What recovery is like, in general terms

A fracture treated in a cast is not managed the same way as one treated with surgery, and a wrist fracture is not the same as a hip fracture. It includes care of the immobilization or the wound and physical therapy once your doctor clears you. There is no single timeline that fits everyone. Report fever, spreading redness, pain out of proportion, swelling that gets worse, tingling, paleness, or trouble moving the fingers or toes.

Before your appointment

Does every fracture need surgery?

No. Many are treated with a cast or a splint. Internal fixation is considered when the fracture is displaced, unstable or cannot be kept in an acceptable position without surgery.

How long does a fracture take to heal?

It depends on the bone, the type of fracture, your age and your health. Timelines are explained at the appointment. There is no single timeline for everyone.

What should I do if the cast feels too tight or gets wet?

Seek care immediately. A tight cast, pain that is getting worse, tingling, paleness or a damaged immobilization should not wait until the usual follow-up.
